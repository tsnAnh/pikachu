# Repository Plan Selection — Verifier Criteria

## Ground Truth Note

Trust repository evidence and observed tool output, not a planner's confidence or self-assessment. A plan may cite only paths, symbols, commands, and behavior supported by the supplied task and repository. Evaluate the requested criterion independently from the other criteria.

## Criteria

### Groundedness {#groundedness}

Score HIGH when every material claim and proposed step is tied to repository evidence, existing paths and symbols, or an explicit discovery step. Score LOW when the plan invents code, APIs, commands, dependencies, or current behavior. Ignore whether the overall design is elegant.

### Root Cause Versus Symptom {#root-cause-vs-symptom}

Score HIGH when the plan identifies and addresses the causal mechanism behind the requested change or failure. Score LOW when it merely suppresses symptoms, duplicates an existing mechanism, or adds a workaround without explaining the cause. Ignore implementation size.

### Scope Discipline {#scope-discipline}

Score HIGH when the plan contains the smallest complete change that satisfies the task and preserves unrelated behavior. Score LOW for speculative refactors, unrequested infrastructure, or missing work required by the task. Ignore sequencing quality.

### Verification Strategy {#verification-strategy}

Score HIGH when the plan names observable checks that would prove the requested behavior and catch regression, including the relevant integration boundary. Score LOW for vague "run tests" claims, checks unrelated to the change, or no way to distinguish success from failure. Ignore code style.

### Regression Risk {#regression-risk}

Score HIGH when the plan identifies realistic compatibility, state, failure-mode, and interaction risks and contains proportionate safeguards. Score LOW when it changes shared behavior without accounting for existing consumers or silently weakens failure handling. Ignore speculative risks outside the task.

### Sequencing {#sequencing}

Score HIGH when prerequisites precede dependents and each step leaves an independently inspectable or testable state. Score LOW when later steps are required to make earlier steps coherent, risky assumptions are deferred, or validation occurs only after an unnecessarily large batch. Ignore prose quality.
